Measurements confirm variable RR intervals, affirming the irregular rhythm typical of atrial fibrillation, as observed in leads I, II, and III. The ST-T changes, particularly the frequent horizontal or sloped deviations in multiple leads like I, II, and aVL, can be attributed to left ventricular hypertrophy and possible ischemic influence. Such changes might suggest strain patterns often seen in hypertrophy, where the ST segment is depressed or the T wave is inverted. These deviations appear consistently along with widened QRS durations, further corroborating hypertrophic changes in the left ventricle. The T wave morphologies across various leads, especially their reduced amplitude and slight inversion, lend support to the presence of these ECG changes, attributing them potentially to ischemic alterations.
