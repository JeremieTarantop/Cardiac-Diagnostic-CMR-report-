Upon examining the ECG, several key features and abnormalities can be identified. The rhythm appears consistent with a sinus or ectopic atrial rhythm, as noted by the regular presence of P waves, although not uniformly across all leads due to variability in amplitude particularly noted in leads such as II and III. A notable observation is the prolonged PR interval, highlighted in several leads with values exceeding 200 ms, indicative of first-degree atrioventricular block. This aligns with a reported heart rate of 52 bpm, suggesting bradycardia. Left ventricular hypertrophy is evidenced by increased QRS amplitudes, especially prominent in leads aVL, V4, V5, and V6, where values surpass 1.5 mV. The presence of ventricular premature complexes is noted, likely contributing to the irregularities in some of the RR intervals, as seen in the sequence with a significantly shortened interval followed by a compensatory pause. In the inferior leads (II, III, aVF), nonspecific T wave abnormalities are observed. Variability in T wave amplitude across beats suggests possible ischemic changes, although these features alone are nonspecific. The findings from lead aVL also suggest potential high lateral involvement, evidenced by altered ST and T wave morphology. Overall, the ECG supports a diagnosis inclusive of sinus bradycardia with ectopic atrial activity, left ventricular hypertrophy, and first-degree AV block, with evidence of occasional ventricular premature complexes and nonspecific ST-T wave changes in inferior leads that may warrant further investigation considering potential ischemic implications.
