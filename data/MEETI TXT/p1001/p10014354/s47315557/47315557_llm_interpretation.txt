Upon initiating the analysis of the 12-lead ECG, the first noticeable feature is sinus tachycardia, indicated by an elevated heart rate of 107 bpm, as observed in the heart rate measurements. Leads II, III, and aVF are crucial in assessing the inferior wall of the heart. The ECG shows ST segment elevation in these leads, suggesting inferior myocardial infarction, which is further supported by the abnormal QRS complex measurements. Leads aVL and I, associated with the high lateral wall, also exhibit changes; specifically, lead aVL shows ST segment elevation, implicating lateral myocardial involvement. In leads V2-V4, which target the anterior region of the heart, there is an indication of poor R wave progression. Although this appears a probable normal variant, it necessitates close attention as it can sometimes signify partial anterior infarction. Low QRS voltages in the precordial leads are present, often indicating an underlying myocardial or systemic condition, such as pericardial effusion or obesity, but are not definitive in this context. The presence of premature atrial contractions (PACs) within the ECG suggests an element of atrial activity disruption but does not independently alter the primary diagnosis. As a result, the combined evidence from ST segment elevation across multiple leads, poor R wave progression, and low QRS voltages, alongside the sinus tachycardia and PACs, strongly supports a clinical diagnosis of an acute ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), with the possibility of a concurrent lateral wall component. This diagnosis, while consistent with typical presentations, emphasises the urgency of further clinical evaluation and intervention to mitigate extensive myocardial damage.
