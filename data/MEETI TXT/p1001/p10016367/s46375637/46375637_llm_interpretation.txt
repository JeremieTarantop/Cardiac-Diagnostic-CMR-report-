This ECG demonstrates a sinus rhythm with a heart rate of approximately 81 bpm, as shown in each of the standard 12 leads. The machine measurements indicate consistent RR intervals, reinforcing the sinus rhythm diagnosis. Notably, an old inferior infarct is evident, primarily suggested by the findings in the inferior leads, II, III, and aVF. In these leads, one might expect the presence of pathological Q waves, which are often indicative of previous myocardial infarctions in the regions these leads cover. Specifically, the significantly reduced QRS amplitude in lead III and aVF supports this, suggesting fibrosis or scarring where previous myocardial tissue may have been affected, leaving minimal electrical activity.

The ST segments in leads II, III, and aVF do not exhibit elevation, which rules out acute ischemic changes, reinforcing the chronic nature of the infarction. Additionally, the morphology and amplitude of the T waves do not indicate acute changes either. In contrast, leads that reflect lateral and other aspects of the heart, such as I, aVL, V5, and V6, do not show significant abnormalities that might suggest lateral or anteroseptal infarctions. This further aligns with an isolated finding in the inferior leads. Furthermore, there is no significant ST segment elevation noted in any leads to suggest acute ischemia or pericarditis, especially notable in aVR which can indicate multivessel coronary disease if elevated.

Overall, the ECG findings support a diagnosis of sinus rhythm with evidence pointing towards an old inferior myocardial infarction. The absence of acute changes such as ST-segment elevation or T-wave inversions in pertinent leads aligns with a stable, non-acute scenario. This reinforces an interpretation of a historical event rather than current acute myocardial distress.
