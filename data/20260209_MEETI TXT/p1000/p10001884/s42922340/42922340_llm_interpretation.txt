These leads present QRS durations above 120 milliseconds, an indicator of LBBB, due to delayed activation of the left ventricle.

Further evidence of LBBB and its implication on ventricular depolarization is visible in the ST-T changes observed across various leads. Additionally, ST-segment deviations in Lead I, seen as upslope formation in the ST segment with some instances of declination, further align with these findings of intraventricular conduction delay. Collectively, these ECG findings provide substantial evidence of an accelerated junctional rhythm with a left bundle branch block, highlighting significant changes in ventricular conduction and repolarization pathways characteristic of this condition.
