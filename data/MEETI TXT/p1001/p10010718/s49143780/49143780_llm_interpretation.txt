In analyzing the ECG image, the rhythm is consistent with atrial fibrillation, characterized by an irregularly irregular ventricular response and absence of distinct P waves across multiple leads, especially visible when scrutinizing leads I and II. The heart rate, calculated around 110 bpm, indicates a rapid ventricular response. The inferior leads (II, III, aVF) display irregular R-R intervals along with alterations in the QRS voltage, which aligns with the rapid response seen in atrial fibrillation. A deviation in the axis towards the right is noted, supported by the appearance of the QRS complexes in lead I being negative and leads II and III showing positive complexes, identifying it as a rightward axis deviation. This observation is further corroborated by the reduced amplitude of the QRS complexes in lead aVL. Upon examining the precordial leads, particularly V1 through V4, signs indicative of an anteroseptal infarct emerge. The absence of R waves and the potential presence of pathologic Q waves in these regions, though challenging to quantify directly from an image, might suggest an old infarction; however, QT intervals and T-wave morphology do not show definitive acute changes that might suggest an ongoing ischemic event. The collected machine measurements, which show a relatively broader QRS duration and increased QTc interval in lead I and other leads, underlines these findings and supports an abnormal ECG reading. These factors collectively provide a basis for the diagnosis: atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response, a rightward axis, and an anteroseptal infarct of undetermined age, reflecting an abnormal ECG presentation.
