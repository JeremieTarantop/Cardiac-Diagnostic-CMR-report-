The presence of high voltage in the precordial leads strengthens the suspicion of LVH.

This elevation suggests acute coronary syndrome, particularly when coupled with the abnormal Q waves in V2, V3, and transient changes in the QTc interval, which further points towards ischemia. The inferior leads show nonspecific ST-T wave changes rather than definitive ischemic patterns, but they do not entirely rule out involvement in an acute scenario. The findings of these acute ST changes clinically raise the concern for an acute ST elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), requiring immediate evaluation and management, potentially in a coronary care unit.
