Upon analyzing the ECG, several key features and abnormalities become evident across multiple leads, which guide the diagnostic process. The overall rhythm is sinus with occasional premature atrial contractions (PACs), as indicated by non-uniform RR intervals and P wave inconsistencies in leads I and II. In assessing the ST-T changes, widespread deviations are present. In leads V2 to V4, the ST segments show an upslope form suggesting myocardial ischemia, a significant finding when accompanied by prolonged QTc intervals, particularly above 500 ms in some beats, as observed in lead V4. This prolonged QTc interval needs to be considered as part of the diagnosis since it typically reflects the potential for repolarization abnormalities.

Leads III, aVF, and II hint towards inferior wall involvement, with lead III showing some upslope ST changes, which might be early signs of ischemia. The high lateral lead aVL also reflects some ST changes, although less pronounced, with some upslope direction, corroborating a possible lateral ischemic process. Lead aVR occasionally presents with upslope ST form and notable inverted T waves, which might point towards extensive ischemia, suggesting a likely subendocardial or diffuse pattern. 

The detailed machine-computed PR intervals and QRS durations confirm atrial electrical irregularities amidst normal QRS widths, excluding typical conduction delays or bundle branch blocks, which affirms the sinus origin with PACs but increases suspicion for an ischemic influence on atrial depolarization. Taken together, the presence of sinus rhythm with PACs, and extensive ST-T abnormalities across various leads, supports a working diagnosis of myocardial ischemia. Such findings warrant further evaluation, possibly with cardiac imaging, to assess the extent and specific regions affected by ischemia to tailor appropriate management strategies.
