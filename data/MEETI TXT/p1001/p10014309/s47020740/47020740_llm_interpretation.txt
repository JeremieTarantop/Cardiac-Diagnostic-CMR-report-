This ECG presents a clear finding of sinus tachycardia, as evidenced by the elevated heart rate of 104 bpm and the consistent RR intervals ranging from 277–307 ms. Sinus tachycardia is characterized by a regular rhythm where the P waves precede each QRS complex, and it is identified across multiple leads with normal P wave morphology and consistent PR intervals, which I observed in Leads I, II, and III. Notably, within the septal leads V2 to V4, there are nonspecific T wave changes. Analyzing the T wave amplitudes and forms in these leads, there are no substantial indicators like deep inversions or pronounced flattening; however, mild changes do suggest some form of nonspecific electrical alteration within the septal region, aligning with the description of nonspecific septal T wave changes. The QTc intervals vary significantly across the leads but generally remain within borderline limits throughout, avoiding any specific indicators of significant repolarization abnormalities. Most PR intervals also fall within acceptable ranges, and the ST segments mostly exhibit an upslope or remain horizontal, without acute elevation or depression that would suggest ischemic events. These findings contribute to an overall impression of a borderline ECG. The absence of pathological Q waves, major ST elevations, or depressions, along with the preserved QRS amplitude interpretations, further supports the diagnosis that the major irregularities are limited to the sinus rhythm variation and isolated nonspecific T wave alterations in the septal leads.
