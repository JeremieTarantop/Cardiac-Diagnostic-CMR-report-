Upon examining the ECG image and computed measurements, several key findings emerge that form the basis of the clinical diagnosis. The ECG reveals an irregular rhythm with absent P waves across the leads, characteristic of atrial fibrillation. This irregularity is supported by variable RR intervals provided in the measurements. Furthermore, the QRS duration is notably prolonged across multiple leads. In Lead I, for instance, the QRS duration consistently exceeds 166 ms, with occasional measurements reaching 190 ms. Such prolongation indicates a significant conduction delay and is a classic indicator of left bundle branch block (LBBB). The wide QRS complexes are also evident in the chest leads, particularly V5 and V6, where QRS durations remain consistently around 180 ms, reinforcing the LBBB diagnosis. The combination of atrial fibrillation and left bundle branch block contributes to the classification of this ECG as abnormal. It is essential to recognize left bundle branch block, as it can overshadow and impede the identification of other cardiac abnormalities, such as myocardial infarction. In summary, the ECG analysis identifies atrial fibrillation, evidenced by irregular RR intervals and absent P waves, and left bundle branch block, marked by the distinctly widened QRS complexes across numerous leads. These findings substantiate the overall classification as an abnormal ECG.
