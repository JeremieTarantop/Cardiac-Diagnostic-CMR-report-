This ECG analysis reveals several notable findings. The sinus tachycardia is evident by the consistently high heart rate of approximately 118 bpm across all leads. Further examination of lead I shows a heightened QRS amplitude, indicative of possible left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH). This finding is corroborated by the increased QRS amplitude in lead aVL, a characteristic feature associated with lateral wall amplification due to LVH. Additionally, leads V3 and V4 display increased QRS amplitudes, which further supports the presence of LVH. 

The secondary repolarization abnormalities observed are potentially linked to LVH and are evidenced by the ST segment and T wave alterations across several leads. For instance, there is a marked declination form of the ST segment in leads I, II, V3, V4, and V5, possibly indicating strain patterns related to hypertrophy. The T wave in these leads often shows abnormalities such as a decline in amplitude or irregular forms, aligning with repolarization changes secondary to the hypertrophy.

The extensive ST-T changes observed in multiple leads may be due to a combination of LVH and possible ischemia. This includes significant ST changes in leads V2-V4, which suggest anterior wall concerns, possibly mimicking ischemic processes but more likely linked to hypertrophy, given the broader ECG context. Overall, the described ECG features align with a diagnosis of sinus tachycardia, LVH with secondary repolarization abnormalities, and widespread ST-T changes that can be attributed to hypertrophy and/or ischemic processes. These insights collectively contribute to what is classified as an abnormal ECG.
