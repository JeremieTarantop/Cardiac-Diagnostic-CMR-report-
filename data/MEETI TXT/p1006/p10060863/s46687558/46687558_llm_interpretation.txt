In particular, lead III shows a very small or even absent QRS complex frequently, supporting this observation. Alongside, abnormal QT intervals, especially prolonged QTc in these leads, could suggest underlying myocardial issues.

Leads V1-V4 show signs of anterior infarct possibility, with lead V2 and V3 presenting pronounced ST segment deviations and T wave morphologies typical for an anterior myocardial infarction (MI). The changes in these anterior leads, specifically the presence of pronounced Q waves and altered ST segments, further suggest potential anteroseptal or anterior MI. Lead V1, for instance, shows significant abnormalities in early repolarization phases.

In summary, the ECG suggests an old or age-undetermined inferior infarct and an anterior infarct, justified by the presence of Q waves and ST segment changes, coupled with low QRS voltages across precordial leads. The sinus arrhythmia is evident from variable RR intervals, indicating variability in heart rate. Collectively, these observed features solidify the diagnosis of sinus arrhythmia, combined with possible inferior and anterior myocardial infarction of undetermined age, corroborated by the low QRS voltages observed primarily in the precordial leads.
