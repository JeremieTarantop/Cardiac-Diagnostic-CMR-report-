Upon examining the 12-lead ECG image, I observed several key features that form the basis of the clinical findings. Firstly, the heart rhythm is regular, as evidenced by the consistent RR intervals, with a calculated heart rate of 86 beats per minute. However, a prolonged PR interval is evident across leads I, II, and aVL, suggesting a first-degree atrioventricular (A-V) block. In addition, leads I and aVL reveal a left axis deviation, which complements the possibility of left ventricular hypertrophy or left anterior fascicular block as the QRS complex exhibits increased amplitude and duration, particularly in lead aVL. Right bundle branch block (RBBB) is evident in lead V1, with its characteristic rsR' pattern and a prolonged QRS duration. This corresponds with machine measurements indicating QRS duration over 120 ms in various leads, confirming conduction abnormalities.

Moreover, leads II, III, and aVF display Q wave presence, implicating an inferior wall infarction, while leads V2-V4 show significant ST segment depression and the formation of Q waves, indicative of an anterior wall myocardial infarction. The observations in leads I, aVL, V5, and V6 suggest lateral ischemic changes with altered T wave morphology. Despite the routine ST segment depression in the precordial leads, the pronounced ST segment changes, alongside the presence of pathological Q waves in the anterior and inferior leads, raise the concern for an acute myocardial infarction. Finally, the presence of wide and bifid P waves in lead II suggests left atrial enlargement. Collectively, these findings define an abnormal ECG pattern, revealing conduction delays, past myocardial infarction, acute ischemic events, and structural heart changes.
