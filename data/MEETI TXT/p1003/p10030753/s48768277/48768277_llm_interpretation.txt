This heightened QRS amplitude, particularly in the left precordial leads, is indicative of left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH). While ST elevation can generally indicate myocardial infarction, the diffuse and consistent pattern across the precordial leads without corresponding reciprocal changes in other leads suggests these may be secondary to LVH rather than indicating an acute myocardial infarction. Moreover, the T wave inversions and broader T waves in these leads further support the presence of secondary repolarization abnormalities due to LVH.
