The ECG interpretation reveals sinus arrhythmia and left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) with secondary repolarization abnormalities. Sinus arrhythmia is indicated by the variability of the RR intervals, which range from 275 ms to 368 ms. In Lead I, the high QRS amplitude, averaging around 1.4 mV, combined with QRS durations approaching 130 ms at times, supports the presence of LVH. The QTc interval is notably prolonged, with values exceeding 500 ms in some instances, particularly in Leads I, aVL, and V3, suggesting repolarization abnormalities.

The T-wave amplitudes across the leads are consistent with secondary repolarization changes associated with LVH. In Leads V2–V4, the ST segment shows upslope forms, while the T amplitude variations highlight intricate repolarization, reflecting LVH influence on electrical activity. The lead II to aVF show alterations in P wave amplitudes, which could reflect the mechanical impact on atrial depolarization in the context of increased left ventricular mass.

The extensive ST-T changes observed across leads, primarily characterized by declination forms, correlate with left ventricular hypertrophy and a possibility of concurrent ischemic processes. This is particularly relevant given the noted greater amplitudes in the corresponding leads and the typical burden LVH imparts on myocardial oxygen demand. These findings align with what would be anticipated in a cardiology-focused ECG evaluation, suggesting an abnormal ECG pattern significantly influenced by left ventricular hypertrophy and associated repolarization disruptions.
